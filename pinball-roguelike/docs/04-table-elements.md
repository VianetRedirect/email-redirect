# Table elements: how real ones work and how ours copy them

Written for version 0.5.0 and extended in 0.6.0. Each section says what
the real mechanism does, what we built, and which test proves it. The
layout is generated by `tools/gen_sump.py`, which mirrors the left half
onto the right so both sides always match.

## Layout rules we follow (added in 0.6.0)

From Mission Pinball's layout guides and other builders:

- **Every shot is a straight line from a flipper,** allowing for half the
  ball's width on each side. `shot_map_test` checks this for every
  target. When a part sat on a shot line it was moved: the outer ramp
  flares and valve 2 both were.
- **Modern tables have a fan of 6 to 10 shots.** The Sump's lower level
  now has five: left lane, left ramp, centre, right ramp, right lane.
  The upper level adds the bumpers, top lanes, saucer, valve 3 and lift.
- **Ramp width.** Builders rate an entrance two ball-widths wide as
  medium difficulty and wider as easy. Subtract the ball's width from the
  opening to get the real target width. Ours are 27 px for a 10 px ball.
- **Rubber posts in front of ramp walls** so a near miss bounces back into
  play instead of dying.
- **Upper flippers need an easy feed,** because shots from them are hard.
  Both ramps drop the ball onto an upper flipper.
- **Every shot gets its own arrow insert,** lit when the shot matters.
- **Lane exits should feed the flipper,** not the slingshot tip or the
  outlane. Guards at the foot of each side lane steer wall-hugging balls
  into the inlane.
- **Surround pop bumpers with rubber, not steel,** or the action dies.
  Not done yet: our bumpers sit in the open. Listed as a next step.

## Proportions

- A standard flipper bat is about 3 inches long. A search confirmed only
  that bats range from 1.5 to 3 inches, so treat 3 inches as the common
  size rather than a checked figure.
- Our bats were 28 px on a 270 px table, about 10% of the width. They are
  now 38 px, about 14%, which matches a 3-inch bat on a standard-width
  playfield. Upper flippers are 24 px, a mini-flipper size.
- The ball grew from 8 px to 10 px across so it stays readable on a phone.

## Flippers

**Real.** A solenoid swings the bat up when the button is pressed. Where
the ball sits on the bat sets the shot. A ball near the tip is struck
early in the swing and goes across the table. A ball near the base rides
the bat through the stroke and goes up its own side.

**Ours.** The bat is a moving physics body plus one explicit kick per
press. Kick speed rises from 520 px/s at the base to 900 px/s at the tip.
Direction blends from 2 degrees toward its own side at the base to 30
degrees across at the tip. The kick zone reaches slightly behind the
pivot, so a ball resting where the inlane meets the bat still gets a full
shot.

**Fixed on the way.** The slingshot's lower corner hung over the bat's
base. Base shots were hitting its underside and dying. The corner was
pulled back.

**Test.** `elements_test`: tip shot goes across, base shot goes up its
own side. `flipper_power_test`: every point on the bat would climb 450 to
560 px.

## Pop bumpers

**Real.** The ball presses a skirt around the bumper. That closes a
switch, which fires a coil that pulls a metal ring down onto the ball and
throws it away. The ball leaves much faster than it arrived, even from a
gentle touch. Usually three sit together, fed by the top lanes.

**Ours.** The skirt is a zone 2 px wider than the bumper. Any ball inside
it is thrown straight away from the centre at 560 px/s, with up to 8
degrees of scatter. The coil then rests for 0.1 s. The ring visibly
shrinks and flashes when it fires. Before 0.5.0 bumpers only set the
ball's speed on contact, so they felt like a bounce.

**Test.** `elements_test`: a ball dropped gently onto a bumper is thrown
more than 60 px in 0.2 s.

## Slingshots

**Real.** Rubber-wrapped triangles above each flipper. A hit on the rubber
face fires a kicker arm that slaps the ball away, like a pop bumper.

**Ours.** Only the long face kicks. It throws the ball at 470 px/s with
10 degrees of scatter, close to a pop bumper's 560. Before 0.6.0 it was
240 px/s, which felt like a bounce. The kicker arm visibly pops out. A
fixed exit speed still stops two facing slingshots trapping the ball.

**Test.** `elements_test`: a slow ball leaves a slingshot fast.

## Inlanes, outlanes and their saves

**Real.** Beside each flipper a divider post splits the side of the table
into an inlane and an outlane. The inlane feeds the ball onto the
flipper. The outlane drains it. Tables protect outlanes in several ways:

- A **kickback** fires an outlane ball back into play once, then goes
  dark until relit. Terminator 2 has one in the left outlane, relit by
  shooting targets.
- A **diverting gate** is a slanted barrier that turns an outlane ball
  into the inlane beside it.
- A **ball saver** returns a ball that drains in the first seconds of
  play.
- Black Knight used a magnet save on both outlanes.

**Ours.** Both outlanes have a diverting gate and a kickback.

- Each gate closes for 20 seconds on every launch and again when the drop
  bank is finished, then blinks for 4 seconds and opens.
- The left kickback is relit by the inlane and outlane rollovers. The
  right one, added in 0.6.0, is relit by the drop bank. Both are lit at
  the start of a sector.
- Guards at the foot of each side lane steer balls running down the wall
  into the inlane, so lane exits feed the flipper.

These are the "traps either side that stay closed until a certain point,
or can be closed as a bonus".

**Tests.** `elements_test`: closed gate feeds the inlane, open gate
drains, lit kickback saves, dark kickback drains.

## Drop targets

**Real.** A bank of targets that fall below the playfield when hit. When
the whole bank is down, a coil resets it and the game usually awards
something.

**Ours.** Three targets across the centre shot, with valve 1 behind them.
All three down pays 10 scrap, recloses both outlane gates, relights the
right kickback, and resets the bank after one second. Rubber posts at
each end stop balls slipping round the sides to the valve.

**Test.** `elements_test`: bank pays, rearms the saves, and resets.

## Standup targets and rollover lanes (added in 0.6.0)

**Real.** Standups are fixed targets that light when hit. Rollovers are
switches in lanes that light when the ball rolls over them. Both come in
banks. Completing a bank awards something, then the bank resets. With
lane change, pressing a flipper shifts the lit lamps one lane sideways so
the player can steer the unlit lane under the ball.

**Ours.**

- Three standups at the top of the left lane. Completing them opens one
  unlit valve, once per sector.
- Four inlane and outlane rollovers with lane change. Completing them
  relights the left kickback.
- Three top lanes on the upper level with lane change, between lane
  guides. Completing them makes every pop bumper super for 20 seconds:
  harder throws and triple scrap.

**Tests.** `elements_test`: standups open a valve, lane change moves the
light, lanes relight the kickback, top lanes make super bumpers.

## Arrow inserts (added in 0.6.0)

Lit arrows in front of each shot. Ramps and lanes pulse all the time.
Valve arrows glow until their valve is lit. The lift arrow lights when
the gate opens. They are visual only.

## Ramp and wire rail

**Real.** A ramp is an inclined plastic track. A shot without enough speed
rolls back down it. A shot that makes it reaches the top and drops onto a
wire rail, a habitrail, which carries the ball by gravity somewhere else,
often to an inlane or an upper flipper.

**Ours.** There are two ramps. The right ramp is fed by the left flipper
and drops the ball onto the upper right flipper. The left ramp mirrors it.
Each is a walled lane with extra gravity pulling the ball back down, so a
weak shot rolls back out. A ball that reaches the top while still
climbing rides the wire rail. Since 0.6.0 it keeps the speed it arrived
with and speeds up along the rail, as a ball rolling down a real wire
rail does. Before that it moved at a fixed slow pace.

In 0.6.0 the ramps moved 60 px up the table and their outer flares were
removed. Before that, the two mouths and their flares formed a wall
across the table, and most flipper shots hit it. Extra ramp gravity
dropped from 450 to 250, so a medium hit now makes the ramp.

**Tests.** `elements_test`: a strong shot reaches the upper level, a weak
shot rolls back. `shot_map_test`: the left flipper can make it.

## Spinner

**Real.** A flat plate hung on a wire across a lane. A passing ball sets
it spinning, faster for a faster ball, and it scores on every turn until
friction stops it.

**Ours.** Three spinners: one across the gap between the upper flippers
and one in each side lane, as on The Hobbit's orbits. Spin rate comes from
the ball's speed. It decays over time and pays 1 scrap per half turn.

**Test.** `elements_test`: a fast pass pays several times.

## Saucer (kickout hole)

**Real.** A shallow cup that catches a slow ball, holds it while the game
scores or starts something, then kicks it out. A fast ball skips over.

**Ours.** Upper left, above the left ledge. It catches balls slower than
450 px/s, holds them 0.8 s, pays 5 scrap, and kicks them toward the upper
flippers.

**Test.** `elements_test`: holds, pays, kicks out. `shot_map_test`: the
upper left flipper can reach it.

## Upper flippers

**Real.** Many tables add a third or fourth flipper higher up. The Addams
Family has four flippers, and its left ramp can only be made from the
upper right flipper. Black Knight split the playfield into two levels.
The Hobbit has four flippers with shots from all four.

**Ours.** Since 0.6.0 the upper flippers are 32 px long, up from 24, with
a 20 px gap between their tips. Before, the gap was 66 px, far wider than
the 23 px between the main flippers. The Sump is two levels joined by
two ramps. The upper level has a
pair of mini flippers on the ledges, fed by the ramp rail and by the
plunger. They shoot at the bumpers, the saucer, valve 3 and the lift. The
spinner sits in the gap between them, so a ball falling back to the lower
level spins it.

## Layout references

- **The Addams Family (Pat Lawlor, 1992):** four flippers, a shot
  through the bumpers, a ramp made from an upper flipper. We copied the
  upper-flipper ramp idea: the ramp feeds an upper flipper, which makes
  the next shot.
- **Black Knight (1980):** the first split-level table, with ramps
  between levels and saves on both outlanes. We copied the two levels and
  the protected outlanes.
- **Terminator 2 (1991):** left outlane kickback relit by targets. We
  copied it exactly.
- **Medieval Madness and Attack From Mars:** two-flipper fan layouts with
  a centre toy. A model for later sectors, not this one.
- **Twilight Zone (1993):** two ramps, three pop bumpers, a small upper
  playfield reached by a ramp. A model for a later sector.

## Shot map

`shot_map_test` fires from ten points along each flipper and records what
each shot touches. It runs every shot twice, with the drop bank up and
down, because valve 1 sits behind the bank. Current lower-level fan:

| Point on bat | Left flipper | Right flipper |
|--------------|--------------|---------------|
| 0.45 | Centre: drop bank, valve 1 behind | Centre: drop bank, valve 1 behind |
| 0.65 | Right ramp | Left ramp |
| 0.85 to 0.95 | Right lane: spinner, valve 2 | Left lane: spinner, standups |

Upper level: both upper flippers reach the bumpers, top lanes and valve 3.
The upper left flipper reaches the saucer. The upper right flipper reaches
the lift.
